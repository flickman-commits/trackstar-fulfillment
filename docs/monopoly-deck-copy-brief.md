# Copy brief — Marathon Monopoly partner deck

You are writing the copy for a seven-slide pitch deck. Someone else will design
it. Your job is words only: every headline, subhead, body line and label, slide
by slide.

---

## The situation

Trackstar is creating **Marathon Monopoly**, a limited-edition Monopoly board
game where each of the 22 property spaces is a real, world-class marathon. It
prints once. Races buy a space on the board.

**Who reads this:** race directors and marketing leads at major US marathons.
Smart, busy, protective of their brand, and sceptical of anything that looks
like a sponsorship deck. Many of them have egos about their race's standing,
and rightly so.

**What the deck has to do:** create enough intrigue that they email Matt for a
call. It is not trying to close anything. The call closes.

**The single action we want:** an email to matt@trackstar.art.

---

## What is wrong with the current draft

There is a live HTML draft you will be given a link to. Read it, then largely
ignore its structure. Its problem is density: it was compressed out of a full
website, so every slide carries an entire web section, and no slide makes one
point cleanly.

**A slide should carry one idea.** If a slide needs a subhead plus three
sub-beats plus a stat row plus a caption, it is two slides or it is the wrong
idea. Cut until each slide has a single thing to say and says it big.

Assume the reader gives each slide four seconds.

---

## The seven slides

You may reorder or rename, but you must land these jobs in this order:

1. **Hook.** The board exists and their race belongs on it. This slide is
   mostly the interactive board. It needs a headline and one line under it, and
   nothing else.
2. **What it is.** The product, and how the game actually plays.
3. **Why now.** Why this moment, not any moment.
4. **What a space does for their race.** The benefit, in their terms.
5. **How it gets sold.** Proof we can actually move 2,004 boxes.
6. **Why Trackstar.** Why we are the ones who can pull it off.
7. **Next step.** Email Matt. The board closes September 30.

---

## Hard rules

- **No pricing. Anywhere.** No slot fees, no deposit, no wholesale, no retail
  price, no cost per impression, no dollar figures of any kind about money
  changing hands. Pricing is a conversation Matt has on the call. The one
  exception is Monopoly's own play money printed on the board spaces.
- **Never use em dashes.** Not in any sentence, ever. This is a standing rule
  across everything Trackstar writes.
- **Do not name the licensing company.** Refer to "the company that holds the
  sole rights to create custom Monopoly editions" if you must refer to them at
  all. Protecting that relationship matters more than the sentence does.
- **Do not claim the game is officially licensed.** It is not signed yet. Say
  nothing about licensing status; Matt handles it on the call. Never write
  "officially licensed", "licensed edition", or anything implying a signed deal.
- **Do not name any race as committed.** None have signed. Boston, Chicago and
  New York are aspirations, not partners.
- **American spelling.**

## Voice

- Short, declarative sentences. Big statements, few words.
- Specific claims beat general ones. "20 race partnerships" beats "we have deep
  industry relationships."
- No hype, no adjective stacking, no "imagine if", no rhetorical questions
  except where one genuinely lands.
- Nothing that sounds like it is managing the reader. A previous draft said
  "we would rather tell you that than have you find it out later" and it read
  as schemey. Say the thing, do not narrate saying the thing.
- Nothing redundant. If a fact appears on slide 4, it does not appear again on
  slide 6.

## Source material

Everything below is true and checkable. Use what serves the argument, cut the
rest, and do not invent anything that is not here.

**The product**
- Limited-edition Monopoly board game celebrating the best marathons in the country.
- 22 property spaces, each one a real marathon. Land on one, pay the registration
  fee. Buy up marathons, add aid stations, charge fees of your own.
- Full contents: board, box, custom playing pieces, title deeds, money, cards.
- Six custom pieces: running shoe, finisher medal, gel packet, water cup, foam
  roller, stopwatch.
- Edition One prints once. It is not reprinted with a different line-up.
- All current imagery is concept renders. Board design, artwork and game rules
  are set during a 12-week design period.

**Why now**
- Over 300 custom Monopoly editions exist. Star Wars, Premier League clubs,
  National Parks, Game of Thrones, NFL teams. There has never been one for running.
- Digital fatigue is the defining consumer mood. Tabletop is one of the few
  physical categories still growing.
- Running has the audience, the obsession, and a gift problem every December.

**What a space gets them**
- Their race's name on Edition One.
- Creative approval of their marks.
- 5 complimentary units shipped to their office.
- First right of refusal on their space in any future edition.
- Only 22 spaces exist, and the races on the board define the edition. Being on
  it is being named alongside the others, in print, permanently.

**Attention maths (use the numbers, never the money)**
- 2,004 boxes x 10 games per box x 3 players per game = 60,000 people at the table
- 60,000 players x 2 hours per game = 120,000 hours of attention
- 120,000 hours x 20 looks at the board per hour = 2.4 million brand impressions
- These assumptions are deliberately conservative.

**Where all 2,004 boxes go**
- 110 complimentary copies to the 22 race partners
- 50 to press and influencer seeding
- 400 race partner distribution (races promote to their own lists, purchase
  happens on the Trackstar site)
- 200 units races buy for their own stores
- 1,244 Trackstar direct to consumer

**How it gets in front of people**
- 22 race partners promoting simultaneously. That is 22 email lists behind one
  product in one gifting window.
- Gifted to running and lifestyle media before launch.
- Seeded to running influencers.
- 2027 holiday gift guide placement being pursued now.
- A simultaneous social media launch: all 22 races post at the same time.
- A full product photo shoot, assets handed to every partner.

**Why Trackstar**
- 20 live race partnerships today, including the Marine Corps Marathon,
  California International Marathon and Eugene Marathon.
- Producing, warehousing, selling and shipping physical product is the existing
  business, not a new capability.
- Nearly 3,000 products sold in year one, almost entirely through partnership
  and social media marketing.
- An email list of 10,000 people who have already bought a running gift.

**Real customer reactions** (from an email to the Trackstar list; 15 people replied)
- "I will with 100000% confidence purchase it if you guys make it happen. We
  spend hundreds on race entry fees and gear so why not on a marathon board
  game." — Ricky F.
- "Great idea. I would order for sure. If it is personalized maybe a bit more.
  Jail should be DNF." — Pat
- "Yes, absolutely. So many ideas: new gel disagrees with you at mile 18, go
  back N spaces." — Dave
- Do not quote the prices any of them named.

**Timeline**
- Locking in races: closes September 30, 2026
- Design: October to December 2026 (12 weeks)
- Production: January to October 2027
- On shelves: Holiday 2027
- Board composition locks the day design begins. That is why the deadline is
  real and worth saying plainly.
